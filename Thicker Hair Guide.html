Thicker Hair Guide
Pattern hair loss, explained plainly

Thicker strands, more hairs.

Every food, medicine, device and habit is listed once. Foods are grouped by nutrient and by protein source, with a rating for how much each one can help. Tap a goal to see only what fits it.

Where to start

  1. Get testedAsk for ferritin (iron stores) and vitamin D. Fix a real shortage before anything else.
  2. Pick a proven treatment with a doctorMinoxidil and finasteride have the strongest evidence. Add food and habits around them.
  3. Give it six monthsHair grows slowly. Guidelines check results at around 6 months.

What do you want to change?

Thicker strands means each hair gets wider. Regrowth means more hairs per square centimetre.

Foods by nutrient

Tap a nutrient to see every food that supplies it. Each food stays one card, with all its nutrients shown.

Hair impact is this guide's rating, not a lab measure. High means rich in two or more hair-linked nutrients. Medium means a good source of one or two. Light means a small or supporting source. Food only changes hair much if you are low in that nutrient. With normal levels it maintains hair but will not add growth, and it cannot reverse genetic pattern loss.

Read this first

This guide is general information about pattern hair loss, not medical advice. Sudden shedding, patchy loss or a sore scalp needs a doctor.

Foods were checked against NIH and MedlinePlus pages for which foods supply which nutrient. Those pages give few amounts per food, so none are shown. One claim from the notes was wrong and is left out: root and tuber vegetables are not a source of B12. Plant foods have B12 only when fortified. Dark chocolate and hemp seeds were dropped because they are not confirmed hair-relevant sources.

Figures from the original notes that could not be confirmed, such as exact percentages, laser schedules and a vitamin D target, were left out. Evidence bars on non-food cards show how well each item is supported for hair.
